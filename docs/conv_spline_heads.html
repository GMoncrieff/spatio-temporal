Five Quantile Heads

Everything downstream of the trunk is one object: a per-pixel quantile function Qh(u|x). These five heads build it five different ways, and the differences are small enough to draw.

a naming collision worth one line

This branch uses e1 (lower case) for the previous phase's neighbourhood-covariate configuration — the thing b1 is defined as "e1 plus trunk context". It also uses E1 for the ISQF head in this phase's slate. This document means the slate's E1 and E2 throughout: --head_family isqf and --head_family pwl.

Where the head sits

the plumbing all five share

The trunk is a ConvLSTM over the HM stack plus static layers. Its final hidden state feeds three heads per horizon: a central head, a width head, and a shape head. Those three write one contiguous block of channels, [location, scale, shape…], and a single decoder turns the block into one quantile function per horizon. That decoder is where the head families diverge; everything to its left is identical across all five.

channel layout, one horizon

The construction is a factorisation, and that is its whole character. Bin heights go through a softmax and accumulate into a ladder v running exactly 0 → 1; the ladder is then re-centred and re-scaled so that u = 0.5 lands on the anchor and the span from 0.025 to 0.975 is exactly scale. Shape, location and width are therefore three separate things the network can move independently — and the published triple is a gather, never an interpolation.

Monotonicity is structural: positive bin heights and positive knot derivatives make the rational-quadratic piece monotone for free, so lower ≤ central ≤ upper cannot be violated and nothing needs sorting afterwards. That guarantee is also the suspect. A strictly positive derivative cannot produce a flat segment, so when the target is flat — and between u = 0.1 and 0.6 the observed change spends 0.13% of its value range — the levels collapse toward each other without collapsing onto each other. That is the picket fence: 4.5% of gaps within three quantisation steps against 0.7% exactly zero.

E1 and E2 — the two C0 heads

E1 and E2 share a base class and differ from each other in one thing only: where the ladder is nailed down. Both give up C1 continuity, and both get the same thing back for it — CRPS in closed form. Both cost 16 channels per horizon instead of 29.

one layout for all three families, deliberately — a per-family layout buys an off-by-one that only shows up as a wrong-shaped distribution

worth checking on the first E1 run

On default14 the u-grid spans [0, 1] — the closed form refuses a grid that stops short — so E1's free first knot lands at u = 0, and the central head's zero-initialised residual therefore starts the run with persistence as the distribution's floor rather than its median. Under b1 and E2 the same initialisation puts persistence at the middle. The phase's second measured defect is 15% of cells displaced one bin left, across zero; E1 begins from the opposite prior. That is the paper's arrangement working as designed, not a bug — but it is the first thing to look at in E1's PIT panel, and it is why the docstring calls E1 a test of the whole parameterisation rather than of one piece of it.

What C0 costs, and what it buys

The density is 1 / (dQ/du). Under b1 that is a smooth function; under E1 and E2 it is piecewise constant, jumping at every knot. CRPS does not care — it integrates Q, not its derivative — but the NLL option and any density render do. What C0 buys is the reason the module exists: with Q linear on a segment, the pinball integrand is a quadratic in u with one kink, both pieces integrate exactly, and the crossing is the root of a linear function.

two channels added; softplus keeps both positive

E1 drops the paper's exponential tails on physical grounds, and the reasoning is correct: q(α) = a log α + b is unbounded on both sides and HM is an index on [0, 1]. But dropping the mechanism also drops the thing the mechanism was for. In E1 the far field is whatever the outermost bin's mass happens to buy, and beyond the last knot the clamp simply holds — a hard stop, with no learned rate of approach.

E1c restores the tail in a coordinate where the bound sits at infinity. Take w = −log(1 − HM); the physical ceiling HM = 1 maps to w = ∞, so Park's exponential tail can be written there unchanged and mapped back. The upper tail becomes

Q(u) = 1 − (1 − qhi) · [ (1−u) / (1−uhi) ]1/βhi

which meets the last knot exactly at uhi, is strictly increasing for any βhi > 0, and approaches 1 without ever crossing it. The lower tail mirrors it toward 0 with Q(u) = qlo (u / ulo)1/βlo. Small β hugs the last knot; large β reaches for the bound.

what E1c does not do

It does not touch the picket fence, which lives in the body. It also costs the property that makes the current module cheap to reason about: one base class, one crps, one code path. Simplicity is a scoring criterion on this branch, and tail_reach20 correlates with pit_ks5 at r = 0.691 across 13 runs — so a tail win and a PIT win may be one axis, not two findings.

E2 discourages a fence; E2a makes one unrepresentable. The mechanism is a reservation. A bin holding probability dpk over value width wk implies density dpk / wk, and wk is scale · hk up to normalisation — so bounding the density is bounding hk from below. Each bin takes its floor off the top; the softmax then shares out the remaining room, and the ladder still sums to exactly 1.

Read the table as three separate questions rather than five options. b1 vs E2 asks whether C1 smoothness was ever load-bearing — it has never been tested, and it is 13 of b1's 29 channels plus the whole quadrature apparatus. E2 vs E1 asks whether the median is the right thing to pin. E2 vs E2a asks whether the fence needs to be forbidden or merely discouraged. E1c asks a fourth question, about the far field, and is the only row here with no code behind it.
