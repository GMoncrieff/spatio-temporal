Seven Quantile Heads

Everything downstream of the trunk is one object: a per-pixel quantile function Qh(u|x). The heads on this branch build it seven different ways, and the differences are small enough to draw.

Three of them can be run today: b1 is the baseline, and E1 and E2 are one flag each. The other four need flags that do not exist yet — --free_scale, --isqf_tails, --isqf_space are proposed names, not names to pass at a shell. Those four are marked throughout, and each still needs a test that it changed something against a seeded control before its arm is run.

a naming collision worth one line

This branch uses e1 (lower case) for the previous phase's neighbourhood-covariate configuration — the thing b1 is defined as "e1 plus trunk context". It also uses E1 for the ISQF head in this phase's slate. This document means the slate's E1 and E2 throughout: --head_family isqf and --head_family pwl. The lettered arms (E1a, E1b, E1c, E2a) are §4.1 of docs/conv_spline_phase.md — the scale group — not sub-cases of E4 or E5.

Where the head sits

the plumbing all seven share

The trunk is a ConvLSTM over the HM stack plus static layers. Its final hidden state feeds three heads per horizon: a central head, a width head, and a shape head. Those three write one contiguous block of channels, [location, scale, shape…], and a single decoder turns the block into one quantile function per horizon. That decoder is where the head families diverge; everything to its left is identical across all seven.

One thing to its left did change, and it is the whole of b1. Under e1 the neighbourhood context reached last_hidden only — concatenated onto the head inputs after the recurrence had already run — so the ConvLSTM never saw "can change happen here at all" and could not combine it with its own spatial and temporal features. It could only have the answer applied to its output. b1 moves the covariate to the trunk and takes it off the heads: one consumer, not three. The precompute-on-the-full-raster requirement (radii ≥ 30 px saturate inside a 128 px chip) is about where the covariate is derived, not where it is consumed, so the same precomputed tensor feeds the trunk unchanged.

channel layout, one horizon

The construction is a factorisation, and that is its whole character. Bin heights go through a softmax and accumulate into a ladder v running exactly 0 → 1; the ladder is then re-centred and re-scaled so that u = 0.5 lands on the anchor and the span from 0.025 to 0.975 is exactly scale. Shape, location and width are therefore three separate things the network can move independently — and the published triple is a gather, never an interpolation.

Monotonicity is structural: positive bin heights and positive knot derivatives make the rational-quadratic piece monotone for free, so lower ≤ central ≤ upper cannot be violated and nothing needs sorting afterwards. That guarantee is also the suspect. A strictly positive derivative cannot produce a flat segment, so when the target is flat — and between u = 0.1 and 0.6 the observed change spends 0.13% of its value range — the levels collapse toward each other without collapsing onto each other. That is the picket fence: 4.5% of gaps within three quantisation steps against 0.7% exactly zero.

E1 and E2 — the two C0 heads

E1 and E2 share a base class and differ from each other in one thing only: where the ladder is nailed down. Both give up C1 continuity, and both get the same thing back for it — CRPS in closed form. Both cost 16 channels per horizon instead of 29.

one layout for all three families, deliberately — a per-family layout buys an off-by-one that only shows up as a wrong-shaped distribution

where E1 puts persistence

On default14 the u-grid spans [0, 1] — the closed form refuses a grid that stops short — so E1's free first knot lands at u = 0, and _decode does not pass hm_t0 separately: the central head's zero-initialised residual therefore starts the run with persistence as the distribution's floor rather than its median. Under b1 and E2 the same initialisation puts persistence at the middle. The phase doc records this as a deliberate decision rather than an oversight — E1's whole purpose is to test the paper's arrangement, and moving the skip to the median would leave that arrangement untested. It is what makes E1b and E2a a controlled pair; see Figure 8.

What C0 costs, and what it buys

The density is 1 / (dQ/du). Under b1 that is a smooth function; under E1 and E2 it is piecewise constant, jumping at every knot. CRPS does not care — it integrates Q, not its derivative — but the NLL option and any density render do. What C0 buys is the reason the module exists: with Q linear on a segment, the pinball integrand is a quadratic in u with one kink, both pieces integrate exactly, and the crossing is the root of a linear function.

E1 vs E1c — the paper, put back in two steps

a 2×2, and why the fourth cell runs whatever the others say

E1 is Park et al. with two things taken out. The exponential tails were dropped on physical grounds — they are unbounded and HM is not — and the paper's free per-horizon scale was replaced by this project's cumulative-in-horizon width. E1c puts both back: the paper as published, adapted only where the ConvLSTM forces it.

Each departure is also its own arm. E1a restores the tails alone, E1b restores the scale alone, and the four runs are a complete 2×2 over {learned tails, free scale}.

The first half: a learned tail rate

E1 ends its ladder at the outermost knot and lets the clamp hold. E1a fits in a space where unbounded tails are admissible — logit(HM) for the symmetric version, −log(1−HM) for the one-directional version that matches the product's tail question — and extrapolates through exponential tails whose rates βL, βR are trained rather than pinned. Mapping back through the inverse transform respects [0, 1] structurally, which is what makes the clamp redundant instead of load-bearing.

The second half: an emergent width

The other departure is not a tail question at all, and it is shared with E2a — --free_scale is one semantic change with a per-family implementation. On isqf it is a subtraction and nothing else: drop the scale_pre renormalisation and the increments carry the width directly, with horizons carrying independent parameters exactly as the paper's Seq2Seq setting does. ISQFQuantile.from_channels already handles scale_pre=None correctly; what stands in the way is that _decode passes the channel unconditionally. E1b is a decode-site change, not a head rewrite.

the row that has to exist before these arms run

Under b1 the 95% width cannot shrink with lead time — cum = cum + softplus(step) makes it true by construction — so "does the band narrow at longer horizons" is currently unmeasurable, not absent, and no statistic could read otherwise. The free-scale arms make the quantity real for the first time. That row needs writing before they run, computed from triple() rather than from a channel: under --free_scale there is no scale channel to read, so a channel-based implementation would work on b1 and break silently on exactly the arms it exists for.

no scale channel — the 95% width is whatever the increments imply

Removing the factorisation takes more than deleting a channel, and the reason is worth seeing. _cumulative_v softmaxes the heights and then divides by the last entry, so the ladder spans exactly 0 → 1 twice over. Strip scale naively and every pixel gets a span of 1.0 — a broken model, not a free one. E2a replaces both normalisations with raw positive increments and drops the / v_span division with them.

two things that do not survive the deletion untouched

MIN_SCALE loses its referent. It has to be re-expressed as a floor on the span v_hi − v_lo, enforced at construction. Without it CRPS can drive the width to zero across the quiet majority of pixels and anchor + (v − v_mid) silently returns a constant — which reads downstream as a perfectly confident forecast rather than a degenerate one. Worth resolving the number while porting: the comment justifies 3e-5, the constant is 1e-6.

E2a and E4 do not compose as written. gap_floor_heights computes its floor in normalised v units as dpk / (f_max · scale), derived from wk = scale · hk / v_span — the factorisation is load-bearing in that expression and --free_scale deletes both terms. Crossing them is a re-derivation, not a combination: the floor has to be restated on the increments in absolute HM, inck ≥ dpk / f_max, which is the simpler statement of the same physical claim anyway.

E2a and E1b are one comparison apart

Write the two out and they converge almost exactly. E2a is anchor + cumsum(inc) − cumsum(inc)[mid]; E1b is q0 + cumsum(inc). The same ladder, differing in one thing: which knot the free location parameter — and with it the zero-init persistence skip — attaches to.

Read the table as four questions rather than seven options. b1 vs E2 asks whether C1 smoothness was ever load-bearing — never tested, and it is 13 of b1's 29 channels plus the whole quadrature apparatus. E2 vs E1 asks whether the median is the right thing to pin. The width column asks whether the anchor/scale machinery was earning its keep, and it is the one column where the cheapest heads sit. E1 vs E1c asks whether Park et al.'s mechanism transfers — and because that is a 2×2 rather than a comparison, it takes four runs to answer honestly.
